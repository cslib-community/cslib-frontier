# Assumptions inventory

A security theorem may assume named hardness assumptions: Prop-valued definitions documented as assumptions, such as `F.DDH setup`, `DecisionLWE …` or `OneWay f`, including a security notion of a component that the caller supplies ([AGENTS.md, Security theorems](../../AGENTS.md#security-theorems)). This page lists each such assumption that the check below finds a theorem in `Cslib.Crypto` taking, and the hardness assumptions the library defines. It is not complete: the check misses some assumptions, and the paragraphs on its limits below name the known ones. For each listed assumption, the page says where the definition comes from:

- **standard**: a cited source states the assumption, and the docstring cites that source definition. The row gives the bibliography key of [references.bib](../../references.bib) and the locator. *Deviations or relation* names every material difference from the source, such as an arbitrary setup law where the source has an efficient parameter generator. A deviation that strengthens the assumption (more adversaries, more information for them, a dropped restriction that the source relies on) makes it novel, unless a cited source states that form or the library proves it from the standard one.
- **novel**: no cited source states the assumption. Its docstring says that it is novel to this library, and *Deviations or relation* says how it relates to standard assumptions (it follows from one, implies one, or neither) and why it is plausible. A novel assumption may support a result presented as original. A theorem that rests on it names it, and is never presented as a cited result: that would be a stand-in under [Faithful headlines](../../AGENTS.md#faithful-headlines) rule 2.
- **vocabulary**: a generic notion parameterized by the objects whose security it states (games, ensembles, an adversary predicate, a bound), such as `Game.Indistinguishable` or `ComputationallyIndistinguishable`. It becomes an assumption only through the instance that a theorem supplies, and review checks that instance under a cited headline. A primitive's or problem's own security notion is never vocabulary, even in a concrete form against a caller's adversary class and bound, as `PRG.Generator.Secure` is.

A row of any status says "locator not verified" until someone has read its locator at the source. A pull request removes the phrase only when its body records that check (the source's version or URL and what was read there) and its reviewer confirms it at the source. A row that says "status pending" has an open question about its status, named in the row; until it is settled, the assumption does not count as the source's under a cited headline. The Boneh–Shoup locators without that phrase were read in Version 0.6 of the book (January 2023), the Regev locator in the author's 2009 version of the paper, and the Kyber and Dilithium locators in versions 3.02 and 3.1 of their specifications. The pull request that removed the phrase from a row ([#5585](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/pull/5585) for the first rows) records in its description what was read, and its reviewers confirmed it. The [Goldreich1998Fragments](../sources.md#goldreich98frag) locators refer to the printed pages of the author's posted draft of February 27, 1998, of the first volume of *Foundations of Cryptography*, recorded in [sources](../sources.md#goldreich98frag); the published volume was not inspected.

*Nonvacuity* links a test that shows the assumption fails for an insecure instance (for example a fixed group, a zero noise law or a constant function), where the library has one.

[check_hardness_assumptions.py](../../scripts/check_hardness_assumptions.py) reads the built library and result registry after `lake build CslibFrontier CslibFrontierResults` (`--list` needs only `lake build CslibFrontier`). It finds every premise of a public Crypto theorem headed by a Prop-valued declaration whose definition mentions negligibility, an advantage or indistinguishability, whatever the declaration's own name, and checks it against this page. An unregistered assumption fails. So does a standard assumption whose docstring cites none of its row's keys, a novel assumption whose docstring does not say "novel to this library", a "locator not verified" in a row of any status, and a theorem resting on a novel assumption that does not name it, or whose docstring presents a cited result (it cites a key, or its first sentence says "theorem" or opens with a bold title) without its first sentence saying "step" or "helper". The existing gaps are recorded, each with a justification, in [hardness_assumptions_baseline.json](../../scripts/hardness_assumptions_baseline.json), which may only shrink. Pull requests and merge groups enforce the check. `python3 scripts/check_hardness_assumptions.py --list` prints the theorems that rest on each assumption. The audit behind this page is [#5578](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/5578).

The check does not judge whether a definition matches its source, whether a locator is right, or whether a theorem that claims a cited result rests on the assumption that the source uses, with the source's instance. It does not see a cited result that a docstring names without a bibliography key, the word "theorem" or a bold title in its first sentence, nor one that only a theorem's name claims. The step test is syntactic too: an original result on a novel assumption, which the rule allows, is flagged when its docstring cites a key, or its first sentence says "theorem" or opens with a bold title, and then passes only through a justified baseline entry. Review covers all of this ([Review cycle](../../AGENTS.md#review-cycle)).

The reader also misses two kinds of assumption. One is a premise written inline, such as a hypothesis `∀ A, Negligible (advantage A)`, which no named declaration heads. The other is a Prop-valued definition that reaches no security atom (`Negligible`, a constant named like an advantage, or one whose name contains `Indistinguishable`) through Prop-valued declarations within five levels: a bound on a quantity not named like an advantage, such as a success probability or an agreement fraction; an advantage behind a helper that is not Prop-valued; or a security atom reached only through a chain of more than five Prop-valued declarations. Such assumptions are not on this page, and a row for one fails as naming no named assumption. The known cases ([#5578](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/5578) finding 9) are:

- `Cslib.Complexity.MildlyHardOnAverage` and `Cslib.Complexity.MildlyHardOnAverageENat`, which the Liu–Pass converse in `OneWay/LiuPassConverse.lean` assumes (`Cslib.Crypto.exists_oneWay_of_mildlyHardOnAverage_timeBoundedNat` and its companions);
- `Cslib.Crypto.Pseudoentropy.SamplablePair.HasGap` and `Cslib.Crypto.Pseudoentropy.SamplablePair.HasGapWithAdvice`, assumed throughout `Pseudoentropy/`;
- `Cslib.Circuits.Boolean.IsAverageCaseHard`, which the Nisan–Wigderson bound `Cslib.Crypto.PRG.NisanWigderson.abs_card_div_sub_card_div_lt` assumes.

Registering them needs the reader to accept them first, which is a follow-up of #5578.

Names are relative to `Cslib.Crypto`.

### Note on DecisionLWEWithErrors

`DecisionLWEWithErrors` is decision LWE in which the error vector has a joint, possibly correlated, law. It has the form of [FullerMengReyzin2020] Definition 4.5 (`dist-LWE_{n,m,q,χ}`, with `χ` any distribution on `Z_q^m`), p. 13 of the ePrint revision of June 23, 2020. FMR assume that definition for any `χ` in Lemma 4.7, p. 14, and with the uniform source over `[-2^(b-1), 2^(b-1)]^m` as `χ` in Theorem 4.11, p. 17. The assumption is novel to this library: `DecisionLWE` is its product case, and for other laws, neither it nor Definition 4.5 at a prime modulus is proved or cited to imply the other. It deviates from Definition 4.5 in four ways.

1. **Modulus.** The modulus `2 ^ w`, possibly superpolynomial, replaces Definition 4.5's prime `q = poly(n)`, which FMR need for decoding (footnote 2, p. 13). The two ranges meet only at `q = 2`, and FMR's hardness evidence (Theorems 4.6 and 4.8, pp. 13–14; Theorem 5.2, p. 18) is for prime `q`.
2. **Distinguishers.** Uniform strict PPT distinguishers with negligible advantage, in place of Definition 4.5's concrete `(ε, s_sec)`-security against distinguishers of size `s_sec`.
3. **Dimension.** The theorem chooses the dimension: `FuzzyExtractor.computationallySecure_lweFuzzyExtractor_of_decisionLWEWithErrors` assumes it in dimension `l = n − k`, as Lemma 4.7 and the proof of Theorem 4.11 do; Theorem 4.11's statement writes dimension `n`, which hardness in dimension `n − k` implies.
4. **Security parameter.** `n`, `m` and `w` are arbitrary functions of a separate security parameter `κ`, where Definition 4.5 takes `n` as the security parameter and `m, q = poly(n)`. A distinguisher's clock is polynomial in the length of its encoded input, framed unary `κ` followed by the `m κ * (n κ + 1) * w κ` bits of the samples `(A, b)`, which need not be polynomial in `κ`. Under the polynomial-time unary schedules that `FuzzyExtractor.computationallySecure_lweFuzzyExtractor_of_decisionLWEWithErrors` requires, the clock is polynomial in `κ`, so a family whose dimension grows slowly in `κ` (say `n κ = ⌊log κ⌋`) asks for hardness against adversaries superpolynomial in the dimension, more than standard LWE asks.

It is plausible as the problem that FMR study, at the modulus of `DecisionLWE`, its product case: FMR derive the prime-modulus form from standard LWE for uniform-interval and symbol-fixing sources. No such derivation at modulus `2 ^ w` is proved or cited, and hardness for particular sources is not proved here. The docstring of `DecisionLWEWithErrors` gives the same account, with an error law for which the two moduli differ.

### Note on CircularCorrelationRobust

`CircularCorrelationRobust hash` is tweakable circular correlation robustness of a hash family on words, `hash : List Bool → List Bool → List Bool`, in the form of [ZahurRosulekEvans2015] Definition 1, §4.1, p. 10, in the ePrint version 2014/756 of March 16, 2015. At level `k` a label is a `(k + 1)`-bit key and a colour bit, and a tweak has `k` bits; `Garbling.CCR.atLevel hash k` reads the hash on them. The offset is `Garbling.CCR.offset R = (R, true)`, whose colour is the least significant bit that Definition 1 fixes to `1`. At level `k` the advantage of a distinguisher is, by definition, the finite `Garbling.CCR.advantage` (`CircularCorrelationRobust.negligible_advantage`).

The assumption has two parts. The premise `polyTime`, deterministic polynomial-time evaluation of the hash on all words, is separate: Definition 1 gives the adversary unrestricted access to `H` but asks nothing of its running time, so it does not supply this premise. The premise is kept because the garbling schemes built on the hash must be efficient. For a hash with that premise, Definition 1 implies the security condition `secure`, since each of its differences from Definition 1 admits fewer adversaries or fixes a parametrization:

1. **Legality.** An adversary asks each tweak at most once on every run (`Garbling.CCR.IsTweakUnique`), at every level. Definition 1 forbids only asking one `(x, i)` with both bits.
2. **Tweaks** are `k`-bit strings at level `k`, not integers as in Definition 1 (`H : {0,1}^k × ℤ → {0,1}^k`, p. 8).
3. **Lengths.** Labels have `k + 2` bits at level `k`, where Definition 1 has `k`.
4. **Adversaries** are uniform strict oracle-PPT word programs, where Definition 1 has polynomial-time adversaries.

[GuoKatzWangYu2020] gives the concrete tweakable form, with repeatable tweaks and every key law of sufficient min-entropy; [ChoiKatzKumaresanZhou2012] gives the dual-key notion it comes from, for non-uniform adversaries. [ZahurRosulekEvans2015] Definition 3 (p. 11), for naturally derived keys, is weaker than Definition 1, and their Theorem 1 (p. 12), privacy of half-gates garbling, assumes only Definition 3.

## UOV and anonymous-credential game instances

The following finite experiments define the problems to which future hardness premises apply. They introduce no new unproved axiom and do not assert a security level. Resource-indexed envelopes use the existing `Game.SuccWithin`; its admitted adversary predicate must account for time and memory as well as signing queries before a computational hardness claim is made.

- **Ordinary UOV:** instantiate `Signature.targetExperiment` with `UOV.idealRawScheme` and independent uniform field-vector targets. [UOV2026], Appendix A, Definition 2, printed p. 49, is the source problem. The current executable language covers finite classical programs; the source assumption additionally quantifies over efficient quantum algorithms. Ideal key generation is distinguished from source seed expansion, and `none` preimage replies reject without conditioning away failure.
- **Multiple-target and one-more UOV:** instantiate `Signature.multiTargetExperiment` or `Signature.oneMoreExperiment` with independent uniform target vectors. [PoMFRIT2026], Definition 3.1, printed p. 7, gives the fixed-budget game: at most `ell` arbitrary-target oracle calls, including repetitions and failures, and `ell + 1` valid answers at distinct indices. Distinct indices may carry equal target values. An unqueried-target freshness game is different and admits the explicit homogeneous rescaling attack in `UOV.Attacks`.
- **Structured ACT certification:** `AnonymousToken.extractedExperiment` lets the adversary choose semantic messages and openings and asks for a valid certificate whose semantic message appeared in no signing request, including failed requests. For the ACT-MQ target `(F(r) + w, G(r))`, a direct hardness premise would be **novel and protocol-specific**; the pinned [VOLEACT2026] Proof status section does not derive it from ordinary or random-target one-more UOV. The library proves only finite extracted-witness reductions and accounting steps here; `AnonymousToken.Conservation` consumes the same chosen-target EUF-CMA term through `extractedConservationExperiment_succProbability_le`, so its bound is informative only where this chosen-target term is small. Raw UOV is not assumed to satisfy target EUF-CMA.

Full adaptive extraction, multi-issuer and random-oracle realization, privacy, and runtime certificates remain [#7068](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/7068); the concrete UOV signer and its failure/retry analysis remain [#4987](https://github.com/cslib-community/cslib-frontier/issues/4987).

## Generic vocabulary

These notions state the security of whatever the caller supplies: two games or ensembles, an adversary predicate, a resource budget or a bound. They are not assumptions by themselves, and a nonvacuity test belongs to an instance, not to the notion. Under a cited headline, review checks that the instance a theorem supplies is the source's assumption ([AGENTS.md, Security theorems](../../AGENTS.md#security-theorems)): `ComputationallyIndistinguishable` of a scheme's keys and an ideal key law, for example, is a premise about the scheme's own algorithms.
